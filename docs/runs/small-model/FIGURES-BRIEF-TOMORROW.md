# Figures for tomorrow's progress talk

Four figures. The data and the four claims are fixed. **How each figure looks is yours to
decide** — pick the chart type, encoding and layout that makes the claim land on a projector at the
back of a room. What is not yours to decide is the brand and the minimalism, below.

## What you are given

- `docs/runs/small-model/figures/`: `headline.csv`, `per-exercise.csv`,
  `candidates-size-vs-accuracy.csv`, `footprint.csv`, `window-length-sweep.csv`, generated by
  `.venv/bin/python scripts/export_progress_tables.py`
- `docs/runs/small-model/`: `fit.json`, `baselines.json`, `parity.json`, `target.json` — the run
  outputs behind those CSVs, with the commands that produced them
- `model/small/` — the exported models (C and joblib) currently shipped
- `docs/RESULTS.md` — earlier sweeps, if you want an extra series
- Chart 3 needs a small experiment that does not exist yet. Its method is fixed; see below.

## The four claims

You choose the wording and the visual. Each figure carries exactly one of these.

1. **Judging complete repetitions beats judging time slices.** 3 s windows 80.9%, per-repetition
   logistic regression 86.5%, per-repetition model 89.3%, same sensors and same data.
2. **89.3% on people the model has never seen, from a model with 1,450 nodes and 31 features.**
   The shipped 107,528-node forest scored 88.3%.
3. **Degraded sensor input leaves the ranking of sessions intact.** Accuracy and rank correlation
   across four severity tiers.
4. **All of it runs on the board.** 96,164 B of a 262,144 B flash budget, 22,824 B of 32,768 B RAM.

## Creative freedom, with one test

- Any honest encoding is fair: dot plot, slope chart, a single large number with one reference
  mark, small multiples, a strip of paired numbers. You are not required to use bars.
- You may drop an axis if direct labels make it unnecessary, and you may make one number enormous
  if that number is the claim. A figure that is one big "89.3%" and a thin reference line is a
  legitimate answer to claim 2.
- Test each figure at half size: if the claim is not readable at 50% zoom, the design is wrong, not
  the reader.
- Keep about 60% of the canvas as data or as the single number. If you find yourself adding a third
  colour, a legend or a second y-axis, the figure is trying to say two things; split it or drop the
  weaker half.

## Minimal, without exception

- One claim line per figure, short. No subtitle and no explanatory sentence inside the image.
- At most three text labels in the plot area, annotations included.
- Two colours: Ink plus either Clay or Oat. Never three series.
- No legend, no value label on every bar, no frames, no shadows, no gradients, no pie charts, no
  chart junk. Faint horizontal hairlines at 8% Ink are the only grid allowed.
- Five ticks maximum per axis, units in the axis label only.
- 1600 × 900 at 2×, Paper ground, at least 15% margin on every side.

## Chart 3's experiment — method fixed, visual yours

1. Keep `model/small/form_rep.joblib` and `model/small/form_window.joblib` frozen. No retraining.
2. Degrade only held-out **test** input with `scripts/augmentation_pipeline.py --generate-only`,
   using the fields in `configs/augmentation-example.json` (orientation ≤15°, bias ±0.02 g, gain,
   gaussian noise, one-pole low-pass 20 Hz, ADC step 0.001017 g, clip 3 g). Four tiers: clean, mild,
   moderate, severe — scale one severity axis per tier and state exactly which. Fixed seed, recorded.
3. Score each tier through the unchanged feature extraction and models, per-repetition probabilities
   mean-pooled to a series verdict, as `scripts/verify_small_export.py` does.
4. Write `docs/runs/small-model/figures/robustness.csv` with
   `tier, seed, n_series, accuracy_pct, delta_pp_vs_clean, spearman_vs_clean, auc_vs_clean`.
   `spearman_vs_clean` is the rank correlation of P(wrong) between the clean and degraded arms over
   the same held-out series — that is the "same ordering" claim.
5. Report what lands. If accuracy falls more than 5 points or Spearman drops below 0.8 at the severe
   tier, say so in the report and soften the claim; do not drop the tier.
